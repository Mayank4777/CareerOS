# Purpose

This document defines the core problem CareerOS exists to solve.

It explains why the product should exist, the problems experienced by its target users, and the opportunity CareerOS aims to address.

This document intentionally focuses on user problems rather than product features or technical implementation. Every future product decision should support solving the problems defined here.

---

# Problem Overview

For most Computer Science and IT students, securing their first software job is no longer a simple application process—it is a long, fragmented, and highly competitive journey.

Throughout this journey, students interact with multiple disconnected platforms and tools, including:

- LinkedIn
- Naukri
- Indeed
- Internshala
- Company career portals
- GitHub
- Resume PDFs
- Google Docs
- Gmail
- ChatGPT
- YouTube
- Learning platforms
- Personal notes and spreadsheets

Each platform solves only one part of the process.

None of them maintains a complete understanding of the user's career journey, progress, or readiness.

As a result, students spend more time managing information than making informed career decisions.

---

# Core Problem

Early-career software professionals lack a unified system that understands their career journey, identifies what is preventing them from getting hired, and guides them toward the next best action.

Instead of making informed decisions based on evidence, users often rely on trial and error, resulting in repeated mistakes, wasted effort, and missed opportunities.

CareerOS exists to replace fragmented career management with continuous visibility, personalized insights, and actionable guidance.

---

# Why This Problem Exists

The modern hiring process is distributed across many independent platforms.

Career information becomes scattered across resumes, job portals, coding profiles, emails, interview notes, certificates, and learning platforms.

Because this information is disconnected, users struggle to answer critical questions such as:

- Am I actually improving?
- Why am I not receiving interview calls?
- Which skills should I prioritize next?
- Which resume version performs better?
- Which companies am I most competitive for?
- Where am I consistently losing opportunities?
- What should I do today to improve my chances of getting hired?

Without a unified understanding of their career journey, users make decisions based on assumptions rather than measurable progress.

---

# Existing Workflow

A typical student manages career information across many independent systems.

For example:

- Resume versions stored as PDFs
- Projects hosted on GitHub
- Job applications spread across multiple job portals
- Certificates stored in cloud drives
- Interview notes written in Google Docs or Notion
- Emails managed in Gmail
- Learning progress tracked mentally or across different platforms
- Career advice collected from AI tools and online content

The user is responsible for connecting all of this information manually.

No existing platform provides a complete picture of their career readiness or recommends what they should improve next.

---

# Limitations of Existing Solutions

Current career tools solve isolated problems.

For example:

- Job portals help users discover opportunities.
- Resume builders generate resumes.
- GitHub showcases technical work.
- Learning platforms teach individual skills.
- AI assistants answer questions.
- Spreadsheets help users manually track applications.

While useful individually, these tools do not communicate with one another or provide continuous career guidance.

Users are left to manually collect information, identify patterns, and determine what actions will have the greatest impact.

---

# Major Challenges Faced by Users

Students and recent graduates commonly experience the following challenges:

### Fragmented Career Information

Career-related data is spread across multiple platforms, making it difficult to understand overall progress.

### Lack of Personalized Guidance

Most users do not know what they should improve next or which activities will meaningfully increase their chances of getting hired.

### Manual Tracking

Users who actively apply for jobs often submit dozens of applications every week.

Maintaining manual application trackers quickly becomes repetitive, inaccurate, and difficult to sustain.

### Limited Visibility into Application Performance

Many users cannot identify where opportunities are being lost.

For example:

- Resume screening
- ATS filtering
- Online assessments
- Technical interviews
- HR interviews

Without understanding these patterns, users often repeat ineffective strategies.

### ATS Screening

Many companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to filter resumes before a recruiter reviews them.

Poor formatting, inconsistent terminology, or missing skills relevant to a job description can prevent otherwise qualified candidates from reaching the interview stage.

Most students receive little or no feedback when this occurs, making it difficult to improve future applications.

---

# Business Impact on Users

Because users lack a unified career system, they often:

- Apply for unsuitable roles.
- Miss opportunities due to poor organization.
- Lose opportunities during ATS screening without understanding why.
- Repeat unsuccessful application strategies.
- Spend time improving low-impact skills.
- Forget previous interview feedback.
- Lose track of applications.
- Feel uncertain about whether they are becoming more employable.

These problems increase frustration, reduce confidence, and significantly extend the time required to secure employment.

---

# Opportunity

CareerOS aims to become the intelligence layer above existing career platforms.

Instead of replacing LinkedIn, Naukri, GitHub, resume builders, or learning platforms, CareerOS connects career information across these tools and transforms it into personalized guidance.

The goal is to help users answer three fundamental questions:

- Where am I today?
- What is preventing me from reaching my goal?
- What should I do next?

---

# Product Direction

CareerOS is built around three core capabilities.

## 1. Collect

CareerOS should minimize manual data entry by allowing users to capture and synchronize career information from multiple sources.

Possible collection methods include:

- Browser-assisted job capture for opportunities the user intentionally chooses to save.
- Resume uploads and resume version history.
- GitHub repositories.
- Future integrations with supported career platforms.
- Manual entry only when automatic capture is unavailable.

The product should prioritize user-controlled data capture over fragile automated scraping techniques.

---

## 2. Understand

CareerOS continuously analyzes collected information to understand:

- Career readiness
- Skill gaps
- Resume effectiveness
- Application trends
- Interview performance
- Career progress
- Areas requiring improvement

The system should continuously learn from the user's journey rather than treating every application as an isolated event.

---

## 3. Guide

Using the collected information and generated insights, CareerOS helps users:

- Decide their next best action.
- Improve resume quality.
- Prepare for interviews.
- Prioritize skill development.
- Learn from previous applications.
- Track career growth.
- Increase their chances of securing relevant opportunities.

CareerOS supports decision-making rather than replacing it.

---

# Success Criteria

CareerOS successfully solves this problem when users can confidently answer:

- Where am I in my career journey?
- What is currently holding me back?
- Which opportunities best match my current profile?
- Where am I losing opportunities, and what can I improve?
- What should I do today to move closer to my career goal?

---

# Scope Clarification

CareerOS is not:

- A job portal.
- A resume builder.
- A LinkedIn replacement.
- A generic AI chatbot.
- A learning platform.

CareerOS is a career operating system that collects career information, understands career progress, and guides users toward better career decisions.

---

# Problem Statement Summary

The biggest challenge facing early-career software professionals is not simply finding jobs—it is understanding how to become employable in an increasingly competitive hiring process.

CareerOS solves this problem by becoming the single system that continuously collects career information, understands career progress, identifies opportunities for improvement, and guides users toward their next best career decision.
